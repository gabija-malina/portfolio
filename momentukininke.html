01

Artist statement

“A collector of moments.”

A friend once called me a momentukininkė - a made-up Lithuanian word meaning “a collector of moments.” I think that describes me and my work perfectly. I love preserving emotions, fleeting expressions and quiet glances: the warmth of a room, the energy of a conversation, or the quiet feeling of being close to someone.

02

Inside the gallery

A room filled with people I wanted to remember.

Each portrait holds more than a likeness. It holds the time I painted it, the conversations around it, and the feelings I had while creating it.

Momentukininkė brings together portraits of people who are deeply important to me. I began focusing on loved ones simply because I love drawing people.

When my mother first encouraged me to draw faces at thirteen, it felt almost impossible. Returning to portrait drawing changed that fear into curiosity, and eventually into the most personal part of my practice.

03

Selected works

A portrait timeline,
from first studies to Busan.

This exhibition brings together portraits from different eras of my life, each reflecting a distinct phase of my artistic journey.

2024–2025

In Korea, I returned to oil pastels (a medium I hadn't used in years) and created fresh, expressive works. More recently, I've been experimenting with acrylics on canvas board, painting raw, emotional portraits of my friends and parents.

2023 August

Just before moving to Korea, I drew my grandparents - a few of the most emotionally meaningful portraits I've ever done.

2023 March

While balancing work and studies, I created a series of ballpoint pen portraits of my friend, drawn during quiet hours at the library where I worked.

2016–2017

My earliest gouache paintings of faces and some charcoal and soft pastel works. These were my first steps into portraiture - awkward, careful, and full of discovery.
