Momentukininkė – Solo Exhibition in Busan, South Korea

Most of the works in this exhibition are portraits, and many of them feature people who are deeply important to me. I only recently decided to focus on painting my loved ones, simply because I love drawing people.

I remember when my mom first encouraged me to learn how to draw faces when I was around 13. I had just finished art school, and the idea felt incredibly intimidating - almost impossible. I thought I'd never be able to do it because it seemed so difficult. But then I returned to that same art school and started focusing on portrait drawing. To my surprise, I actually enjoyed it a lot.

A friend once called me a momentukininkė - a made-up Lithuanian word, meaning "a collector of moments." I think that describes me and my work perfectly. I love preserving emotions, fleeting expressions, and quiet glances. I want to catch the warmth of a room, the energy of a conversation, or the quiet feeling of being close to someone.

Each portrait in this exhibition holds more than just a likeness. It holds a memory — the time I painted it, the conversations that happened around it, the feelings I had while creating it. It's more than a photograph. It's a collection of experiences and connections, encapsulated in a single piece. That's what makes painting so meaningful to me.

Timeline

This exhibition brings together portraits from different eras of my life, each reflecting a distinct phase of my artistic journey.

2024–2025

In Korea, I returned to oil pastels (a medium I hadn't used in years) and created fresh, expressive works. More recently, I've been experimenting with acrylics on canvas board, painting raw, emotional portraits of my friends and parents.

2023 August

Just before moving to Korea, I drew my grandparents - a few of the most emotionally meaningful portraits I've ever done.

2023 March

While balancing work and studies, I created a series of ballpoint pen portraits of my friend, drawn during quiet hours at the library where I worked.

2016–2017

My earliest gouache paintings of faces and some charcoal and soft pastel works. These were my first steps into portraiture - awkward, careful, and full of discovery.
